## The model and its uncertainty

The predictor is the published univariate OLS (Study 001): ddG = -0.198 + 0.089 x `vbur_max_delta_qvbur_min`, fit on **10 ligands** with a residual standard error of **0.327 kcal/mol**. Each prediction carries an ordinary-least-squares 95% prediction interval, `yhat +/- t(0.975, n-2) * s * sqrt(1 + 1/n + (x0-xbar)^2/Sxx)`, which widens for ligands far from the training mean.

## Applicability domain

Domain membership uses the OLS leverage against the warning leverage **h\* = 3p/n = 0.60**. **9 of 10** candidates fall inside it. Leverage and the deck's 1-D training-range label can disagree for a ligand just outside the feature min/max; both are reported.

### The honest limit of these predictions

For **7 of 10** candidates the 95% ee interval spans both signs of enantioselectivity - i.e. the model cannot even commit to which enantiomer is favoured. Near the racemate a 0.33 kcal/mol residual maps to roughly +/-60% ee, so those predictions are **not meaningfully falsifiable on ee**. The genuinely discriminating, falsifiable predictions are the high-buried-volume ligands whose intervals stay well above the racemate. The test below is therefore run on ddG, where the interval is informative for every ligand.

## Pre-registered falsification rule

**Primary test** (8 interpolation_maximin candidates):

> The model is **corroborated** iff the measured ddG-double-dagger lies within the 95% prediction interval for at least **6** of the 8 primary candidates **and** the Spearman rank correlation between predicted and measured ddG is **positive**. Otherwise it is **falsified**.

**Secondary test:** boundary-challenge ligands, scored as an extrapolation stress-test; reported but not gating the primary test.

This rule is fixed now and must not be adjusted after outcomes are known. A 95% interval is expected to miss about one candidate in twenty by chance, so the coverage bar tolerates noise while a wrong sign or more than two misses falsifies; the rank clause guards against a flat fit that happens to sit inside wide intervals.

## Experimental protocol

- **Reaction:** Ni-catalyzed homo-Diels-Alder (hDA), asymmetric variant.
- **Source:** Sigman Group, Ni-Catalyzed-hDA repository (https://github.com/SigmanGroup/Ni-Catalyzed-hDA); the univariate enantioselectivity model reproduced in StericX Study 001.
- **Measurement:** Run the asymmetric Ni-hDA reaction with each candidate ligand under the source conditions; determine product enantiomeric excess by chiral HPLC or SFC.
- **Scoring:** Convert measured ee (fraction) to ddG = 2 R T atanh(ee) at T = 298.15 K, then compare to the interval above.

## Limitations (kept in view)

- Ten training points; a univariate steric model of one reaction family.
- Candidates are DFT-characterized, not necessarily synthesizable.
- Must not be refit after any outcome is known.

This is the natural falsifiable hook for outreach: a frozen, dated, hash-anchored prediction with honest uncertainty, and the exact experiment that would prove it right or wrong.
