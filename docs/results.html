StericX physical-organic featurization engine · Rust

Independent reproduction study

Fast steric descriptors, checked against published chemistry.

StericX computes Sterimol, buried volume, and donor pyramidalization from molecular coordinates in Rust. Its strongest result is descriptor reproduction; its current native ligand-ranking model performs below random selection.

The median absolute error is 0.1115 Å³, while RMSE is 0.4906 Å³: the residual has a substantial tail. The full-set result is the primary comparison; no residual-based exclusions are applied to it.

Scaling to the full library also surfaced a real kernel bug the eleven trisubstituted ligands could never trigger: the quadrant frame identified a donor's substituents as its three nearest heavy atoms, which silently mis-framed primary and secondary phosphines (R–PH₂, R₂P–H) by discarding their bonded hydrogens. Selecting the covalently bonded atoms instead — hydrogens included — removed every spurious zero and lifted R² from 0.9649 to 0.9852 without dropping a single ligand (the validated count in fact rose). The residual is heavy-tailed: median error 0.11 Å³, 90th percentile 0.71 Å³, and R² rises to 0.9897 once the thinnest 1% tail is set aside.

A third descriptor class closes even tighter. Kraken publishes two geometric pyramidalization descriptors for the donor — pyr_P (Radhakrishnan) and pyr_alpha (mean out-of-plane angle). Both reduce to closed forms on the donor's three bond vectors, verified against morfeus to machine precision before implementation. Because they depend only on those three directions — not on the metal centre, sphere, or lone-pair conventions that bound the buried volume — the native kernel reproduces the published values almost exactly, mean R² = 0.99998:

Why a practitioner would use it

≈14× faster in the recorded benchmark

Study 008 timed 1,546 pre-existing structures, one per ligand, on one core of an AMD Ryzen 5 5600G. The reported speedup is 13.76× versus morfeus-ml 0.8.0, using the best of three warm-cache runs. Geometry generation is excluded.

The full paired comparison is R² = 0.998549 over 1,534 structures, with a maximum difference of 8.55 percentage points. A secondary comparison excluding 16 discrepancies above 0.5 percentage points gives R² = 0.999999; those cases remain part of the reported full result. The recorded rule requires interval coverage for at least six of eight primary candidates and positive Spearman rank correlation. This is a proposed criterion, not demonstrated predictive success or calibrated 95% coverage. The source response is ddG_abs: a selectivity magnitude with no enantiomer assignment. Seven intervals extend below zero, exposing the unconstrained OLS model's behavior near the nonnegative boundary; they do not predict the opposite enantiomer. The original signed-ee interpretation and mislabeled feature units need the dated interpretation correction when discussing the preserved preregistration.

What it does not claim

  • Compact descriptors don't replace the published one. StericX's own feature set does not reproduce the ten-ligand Ni-catalyzed homo-Diels–Alder selectivity model (native leave-one-out Q² ≈ 0.00) — a deliberate ablation, reported not hidden.
  • Better geometry didn't improve the kinetic model. Higher descriptor fidelity did not raise held-out Q² on that small n = 11 family; the two are decoupled here.
  • A residual tail remains. Tertiary phosphines have near-zero mean signed residual but mean absolute error 0.256 Å³. The 24 P–H-containing ligands show larger positive bias. A coordination-centre convention is a supported explanation, not a demonstrated cause for every residual.
  • Screening is not validated candidate discovery. Study 011's top-1 recovery is below its random baseline, with failed fits and descriptor-aggregation differences included.
  • No measured prospective validation is reported. The ten-candidate forecast uses the published Kraken-feature model and predicts selectivity magnitude. Experimental outcomes and calibrated uncertainty remain outstanding.

Method & attribution

Kraken's DFT geometries were retrieved from the public MolSSI descriptor-library REST API; the API's descriptor values match the published dataset, confirming provenance. Buried volumes use a deterministic voxel grid at Kraken's convention (3.5 Å sphere, Bondi radii ×1.17, 2.28 Å metal distance).
